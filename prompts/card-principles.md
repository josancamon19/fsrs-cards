Grounding: retrieval practice (testing effect), the generation effect (producing an answer beats recognizing one),
the minimum-information principle (one idea per card), and transfer from application (using an idea in a small
problem builds understanding that reciting it does not).

Write:
- Simple exercises with one short or numeric answer that make Joan use the idea ("A die is loaded so P(6) = 0.5 and
  the rest share the remainder equally. What is P(2)?").
- Predict-then-check questions: commit to a guess, then the back shows the result and why.
- Contrast pairs that sharpen a distinction ("Why can a PDF exceed 1 when a PMF can't?").
- One derivation step per card, not a whole derivation.
- One-relation connection cards ("Variance is the MSE when you always predict ___").
- For process or craft topics (how to build an RL environment, how to calibrate something): one open-ended card per
  topic, answered by a short checklist of key points and a self-grade line in Extra.

Avoid:
- Formula fill-ins that only show a name ("Surprise: […]"), or where the visible half gives away the hidden half.
- List or enumeration cards ("Name the 8 branches", "3 reasons why…").
- Vague or pedantic "why" cards whose answer is a slogan.
- Vocabulary or philosophy trivia.
- Ambiguous prompts that have several acceptable answers when only one is graded.

The answer must be checkable in a few seconds. The back explains the result briefly: the number or claim first, then
one or two lines of why.
